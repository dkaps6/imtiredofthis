## Open scientific question

The still-open mechanism is not another directional efficiency correction.

It is:

> Does a player's own strictly-prior game-to-game volatility in realized
> per-opportunity efficiency predict how far his next-game efficiency is likely
> to move away from his own prior mean, after controlling for prior efficiency
> level and prior opportunity volume?

This is a distribution / tail-risk question only.

## What would be genuinely new

V1 uses only:
- stable player identity;
- strictly-prior player games;
- realized per-opportunity efficiency;
- realized prior opportunity volume.

It does NOT use:
- opponent matchup;
- target depth;
- CPOE;
- run location;
- coverage;
- player mean correction;
- model residuals;
- sportsbook data;
- 2026 outcomes.

The advancing feature is prior game-to-game efficiency SD over the last eight
eligible games. Prior MAD is descriptive robustness only and cannot replace SD
post hoc.

If V1 fails, do not rescue it with:
- alternative windows;
- volatility buckets;
- player archetypes;
- WR1 / WR2 / TE1 / bellcow slices;
- matchup interactions;
- alternative tail thresholds;
- position pooling;
- target-depth / CPOE / run-location recombination.

A positive V1 would authorize only a separately frozen excess-noise / proper-
scoring distribution experiment. It would not authorize a mean change.
