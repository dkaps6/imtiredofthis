# Research Component Preservation Doctrine

Status: ACTIVE RESEARCH GOVERNANCE
Date adopted: 2026-09-09
Applies to: RB, QB, WR, TE, joint-game, ATD, and game-market research

## Core rule

A candidate can fail full promotion without every component of that candidate being a failure.

When an experiment improves a majority of the predeclared metrics, cohorts, structural contracts, or football mechanisms but misses one or more gates, the default response is **decomposition, not wholesale deletion**.

The failed full candidate must remain preserved exactly as a failed experiment. However, any subcomponent that is independently supported by the evidence should be treated as a candidate parent component for the next research generation rather than automatically discarded.

## Required workflow after a mixed/failed candidate

1. Preserve the original run, commit, artifacts, frozen plan, gates, and scientific disposition unchanged.
2. Identify which metrics, cohorts, regimes, and structural contracts objectively improved.
3. Identify which specific mechanism, cohort, regime, or activation condition caused the failure.
4. Separate **supported components** from **unsupported components**.
5. Freeze supported components for the next child candidate whenever they can be isolated without changing their tested behavior.
6. Change only the defective or unsupported portion when a football-grounded, pregame-safe hypothesis exists.
7. Re-test the child candidate under the same or stronger scientific protections. Do not relax a failed gate simply to obtain promotion.
8. Do not threshold-shop, cherry-pick seasons, or retrospectively redefine success criteria after results are known.
9. A successful component is not automatically production-authorized. It remains research lineage until the complete child stack clears the required gates.
10. Record explicit inheritance lineage: which parent mechanisms are frozen, which mechanism changed, why it changed, and which prior failure it is intended to address.

## What "lock the good parts" means

It means:

- preserve the mechanics that demonstrably improved projection quality;
- preserve conservation and leakage protections that passed;
- preserve successful cohort/regime behavior;
- use the successful candidate as the parent architecture when appropriate;
- investigate and repair the isolated failure mode.

It does **not** mean:

- promote a failed full model;
- hide a failed season or subgroup;
- weaken the original scientific gate;
- retune coefficients against the failed validation block;
- discard negative evidence;
- claim a component is independently supported when the experiment cannot identify which component caused the improvement.

## R26 example establishing the doctrine

RB R26 Vacancy-Gated R9 Retrospective V1 passed 19 of 20 frozen gates and improved 5 of 6 seasons, including a material Week-1 receptions improvement, while preserving all football-conservation and sportsbook-separation contracts. It still correctly received a no-shadow scientific disposition because 2023 vacancy-incumbent reception MAE worsened beyond the frozen season-protection gate.

The correct interpretation is not "R26 is useless." The supported parent architecture includes:

- non-vacancy rooms remain production-exact;
- finite RB target pool conservation;
- R9 receiving identity as the redistribution identity mechanism;
- non-RB entitlement exactness;
- receiving-yard mean exactness;
- R22 authority exactness;
- sportsbook inputs upstream = 0;
- strict-prior/leakage-safe mechanics;
- broad pooled and Week-1 improvement.

The isolated defect is the coarse activation condition that treated any ACT/INA RB/FB roster exit as equivalent to meaningful vacated receiving work. Subsequent R26B/R26C work therefore investigates exit significance while preserving the supported R26 parent mechanics.

## Handoff requirement

Every future research handoff/result document for a mixed or failed candidate should include, when applicable:

- **FULL-CANDIDATE DISPOSITION**
- **SUPPORTED COMPONENTS TO PRESERVE**
- **FAILED/UNSUPPORTED COMPONENTS**
- **NEXT CHILD-CANDIDATE DELTA**
- **DO-NOT-CHANGE LIST**

This makes the research cumulative instead of repeatedly restarting from the current production baseline.

## Prior-position salvage backlog

The project may have historical QB/WR/TE/RB experiments where a full candidate failed and useful subcomponents were not explicitly preserved under this doctrine.

After the current RB receiving lane is resolved or cleanly bounded, perform a **component-salvage audit** of prior failed/mixed positional experiments. The goal is not to reopen every old migration. The goal is to identify experiments with clear component-level gains that can be isolated from the failure mechanism and reused in future work.

Priority remains the active RB receiving research lane until it is resolved/bounded.
